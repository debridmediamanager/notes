rex questions

Short answers to what people ask before they install rex, and soon after. Each answer links to the guide that has the details.

Before you install

What is rex?

A media server for the official Plex apps that owns no files. Your apps see one more Plex server with posters seasons watch history and Continue Watching. A source tells rex what is in the library and serves each file. rex streams it when you press play. Nothing is downloaded first.

It is its own server. It is not a Plex Media Server plugin and does not need one. Run it beside Plex if you want both. Install rex explains the names and Move from Plex to rex covers both routes.

What does it cost?

rex is free for personal use with no paid tier. Its source code is public under the PolyForm Noncommercial license. Commercial use is not allowed. Every build is public on GitHub Releases except the Android build, which ships only inside the sponsor-only zurg+rex Android app, a Plex server on your phone.

Things around it may cost something:

   
rex-stremio, your own app or an addon as the source Free
zurg nightly, a source for a debrid or Usenet library A perk for DMM sponsors
A DMM API key, for DMM's exact matches in zurg Optional, also from sponsoring DMM
rex's Docker image Free and public, for Linux: rex in Docker

Is my Plex account at risk?

rex is not made by Plex and Plex could act against an account that registers it. Use a fresh free Plex account made for rex then share it with your own account. Your history friends and Plex Pass stay on your own account. Add it to a Plex account walks through it. Use rex at your own risk.

Does it play 4K, HDR and Dolby Vision?

Yes. Files play in their original quality: rex hands the app the file as it is, and the app decides what it can show. When an app cannot play a file as it is, rex converts it, if FFmpeg is set up (Converting video). A conversion turns HDR and Dolby Vision into a standard picture (Settings, Transcoder, Enable HDR tone mapping, on by default), on the graphics chip where it can. With Intel's OpenCL runtime installed, a small Intel machine (a J4105) converts 4K HDR10 and Dolby Vision profile 5 to 1080p at about real time. Without FFmpeg, a file the app cannot play shows "This server is not powerful enough to convert video". When every title says so on every device, FFmpeg is not the fix: see Nothing plays.

Plex for iOS and Dolby Vision. Plex for iOS 2026.20.0 sits at 0:00 when it plays a Dolby Vision file as it is. A real Plex Media Server serving the same file stalls the same way, so the fault is in the app. HDR10 and SDR 4K files play.

Do I need Plex Pass?

No. rex tells the Plex apps that the account it is registered on has Plex Pass, so playing away from home and converting video work with free accounts. Playing away from home was checked in Plex Web with a free account on both sides; other apps have not been tested away from home. Movie editions, the Dashboard, Downloads, intro and credits skipping and the rest of Movie and TV capabilities work too. An app can still apply its own rules.

Can I download for offline viewing?

The Plex apps' Downloads are there. rex prepares the download on its own machine, as the original file or converted with FFmpeg, and keeps it in a folder beside rex.db until the app fetches it. The account rex is registered on can always download; anyone you share with needs downloads allowed on their share (Share rex). The app can still apply its own rules. A converted download leaves text subtitles out. Saving a download on a phone and watching it offline has not been tested yet.

Can several people use it, each with their own history?

Yes. Everyone you share rex with signs in as themselves and gets their own watched state, Continue Watching, ratings and playlists. Each can copy their history from an old Plex server (Bring your watch history). Share rex with family and friends has the steps, managed users for children included.

What hardware does it need?

A machine that stays on, running Linux (amd64 or arm64, glibc 2.35 or newer, so Ubuntu 22.04 and up), macOS (Intel or Apple Silicon) or 64-bit Windows; or the zurg+rex Android app on an Android/Google TV device (64-bit until the 11 November 2026 nightly), which runs zurg and rex together (sponsor-only; see the Android guide).

Memory: give it 1 GB. On a library of 4,062 movies and 656 shows, rex used under 500 MB while it started and about 730 MB while the library was browsed. Converting video needs more on top.

Disk: none for movies. rex keeps its library in rex.db. Downloads and the images analysis makes (seek previews, chapter images) also go beside it, if you use them.

Converting: only with FFmpeg. A graphics chip makes it fast: a 1080p film converted on a small Intel chip starts in about 2 seconds. Which chips rex uses on each system is in By platform: on macOS it always converts on the processor.

Does it read my local files?

No. rex never scans a folder or a disk and needs no mount. Every file comes from a source over HTTP. Files on your own disk can still go in: something has to serve them and tell rex about them, as rex with your own app shows.

Does it work with Usenet?

Yes, through a source that reads Usenet, such as zurg. rex never talks to a Usenet provider: the source reads the files and serves them to rex like any other file. Set up zurg for it with Usenet and point it at rex as in rex with zurg.

Do I need zurg?

No. zurg is one source among others. rex accepts anything that speaks its feed API:

  • rex with your own app: any app that serves files over HTTP
  • rex with your Stremio addons: catalogs from Stremio addons
  • rex with zurg: your debrid account or Usenet
  • Building rex addons: home videos, recordings or channels, with a program of your own, and three tutorials to start from

Several sources can fill the same Movies and TV libraries.

Do I need to forward a port?

Not at home. Plex apps on your home network find rex without a router change. app.plex.tv also works over the HTTPS address rex-direct gives a claimed rex.

rex needs its own address for away-from-home viewing or another household. The simplest route is one port forward (rex-direct). With IPv6 and no public IPv4 use rex-direct over IPv6. Tailscale serve needs no port forward on devices that run Tailscale. Use it outside your home compares the options.

What does it not do?

There is no tuner or DVR. Music Live TV and photos need a source that sends them. See Music with rex and Live TV with rex. zurg sends comic books as photos which Plex Web shows. Plexamp radio mixes and stations are missing. rex has no relay so remote use needs its own address (Use it outside your home).

After you install

Why does the app show a Plex version, and "Check for Updates"?

rex introduces itself to the apps and to plex.tv as a Plex Media Server, so the apps show a Plex Media Server version number. That number is not rex's. Check for Updates always answers that the server is up to date: rex never updates itself. To update rex, download a newer nightly as in Updating.

Why does my browser at port 32400/web not open a library?

rex has no web app of its own. Opening http://<rex's LAN IP>:32400/web shows a short status page. Watch in a Plex app or in a browser at app.plex.tv.

What leaves my machine?

  • plex.tv, at start and once a day, registered or not: the server's id, name and version, to read Plex's public list of releases for the newest version number.
  • plex.tv, once registered on a Plex account: the claim, the server's addresses (its LAN address and its HTTPS addresses) and its library names.
  • Plex's metadata service, once registered: the id of each title, to get its name, poster, summary and episodes. A subtitle search, by Search Subtitles or a person's automatic subtitle setting, sends the file's name and fingerprint.
  • Artwork hosts, when an app shows them: requests for the posters and pictures your sources name.
  • DMM, for rex-direct, once registered and then every 6 hours: the server's id, its token on the Plex account and its LAN IP. DMM checks with plex.tv that the server is yours, sees your public IP and points your rex-direct names at it and at the LAN IP. If the machine has a global IPv6 address, rex sends it too, a minute after it starts (or is claimed) and then every 6 hours, and each time DMM connects to it to check it reaches the same server. Turn it off with REX_DIRECT_URL=off.
  • Your sources, when you play or delete: requests for the files you play, and deletes (below).
  • Your old Plex server, only when you bring your watch history: requests for your watch history.
  • Webhooks, only if you set any: events, to the addresses you set.

rex asks no one what a release is: the source decides and tells rex. zurg, for one, asks DMM (Why DMM places the files).

Does deleting in a Plex app delete the file?

Yes. Deleting a movie or episode in a Plex app, signed in as the account rex is registered on, deletes its files at the source, as a Plex server deletes files from its disk. A source on a debrid account, such as zurg, deletes them there. If the source refuses, the title stays and the app says it could not be deleted. People you share with cannot delete. Deleting in a Plex app says how to turn it off.

Nothing plays, or every app says it is not powerful enough

Look at rex's log right after you press Play. upstream failed or 502 there means rex cannot reach your source at the address the source gave it, and installing FFmpeg does not fix that. The usual cause is zurg in Docker Desktop on Windows or macOS. The upstream failed row of rex with zurg or rex with your own app checks the address and says what to change. If only some files fail, often only in a web browser, the device cannot play them as they are: set up Converting video.

A title is matched wrong. How do I fix it?

The source decides what each file is, and rex shows that title. Sign in as the account rex is registered on and use Fix Match: rex keeps your pick, and a source can read it back to keep it as that release's match, as zurg does (Why DMM places the files).
